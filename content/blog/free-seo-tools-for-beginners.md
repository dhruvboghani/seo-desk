Paid SEO platforms are powerful, but they're built for agencies and large sites. For a small business or a new website, free tools cover almost everything you need: checking pages, finding keywords, watching performance and fixing problems. Here are the ones worth learning first.

## The essentials

### Google Search Console

**What it does:** shows how your site performs in Google search. Clicks, impressions, average position and the exact searches people used to find you. It also reports indexing problems, Core Web Vitals, mobile usability issues and security problems.

**Why first:** it's data straight from Google about your own site, and no other tool has it. Add your site, verify it and submit your [sitemap](/blog/xml-sitemap-guide).

### Google Business Profile

**What it does:** your listing on Google Maps and local results, with calls, directions and reviews.

**Why:** for any business with local customers, it's often the biggest source of enquiries. See our [setup guide](/blog/google-business-profile-guide).

### Google PageSpeed Insights

**What it does:** measures how fast your pages load for real Chrome users and in a lab test, and lists what slows them down. Our [Core Web Vitals guide](/blog/core-web-vitals-guide) explains the results.

### Rankdesk

**What it does:** the [SEO audit](/seo-audit) checks a page's titles, meta tags, headings, schema, redirects, robots.txt, sitemap and PageSpeed in one go. The generators write the fixes: [meta tags](/meta-tag-generator), [schema](/schema-generator), [hashtags and bios](/hashtag-generator), [keyword ideas](/keyword-ideas), [robots.txt and sitemaps](/robots-txt-sitemap-generator).

## For keyword research

- **Google Keyword Planner:** search volume ranges and keyword ideas, free inside a Google Ads account, without running ads.
- **Google Trends:** compare search interest over time and by state. Great for seasonal businesses.
- **Google autocomplete and "People also ask":** real searches, straight from the results page.
- **AnswerThePublic:** groups questions people ask around a keyword. The free tier has daily limits.

Read our [keyword research guide](/blog/keyword-research-for-beginners) for how to use them together.

## For checking your work

- **Rich Results Test:** confirms whether your schema qualifies for rich results.
- **Schema Markup Validator:** checks any schema.org markup for errors.
- **Facebook Sharing Debugger and LinkedIn Post Inspector:** preview and refresh how your links look when shared.
- **Bing Webmaster Tools:** Search Console for Bing, which also feeds other search engines. It includes its own site scanner.

## For whole-site checks and links

- **Screaming Frog SEO Spider:** a desktop crawler that checks every page for broken links, missing titles, redirects and more. Free for up to 500 URLs, which covers most small business sites.
- **Ahrefs Webmaster Tools:** free site audits and backlink data for sites you verify as your own.
- **Ahrefs Backlink Checker:** a quick look at the top links to any site, including competitors.

## For images

- **Squoosh:** compress images and convert to WebP or AVIF in your browser. See our [image SEO guide](/blog/image-seo-guide).

## A simple weekly routine

**Every week (15 minutes)**
1. Search Console: check clicks and impressions, and any new errors.
2. Business Profile: reply to new reviews and questions.

**Every month (1 hour)**
1. Audit your five most important pages.
2. In Search Console, find searches where you rank on page two and improve those pages.
3. Check PageSpeed for your home page and a key product or service page.

**Every quarter**
1. Crawl the whole site with Screaming Frog for broken links and missing tags.
2. Refresh your keyword list and plan new pages.

## When to pay

Consider paid tools when you need rank tracking for many keywords, deep competitor research or backlink analysis across a whole industry. Until then, these free tools, used consistently, will take you a long way. Our directory of [free SEO tools](/free-seo-tools) links to all of them.
