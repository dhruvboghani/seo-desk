Keyword research means finding the words people type into Google when they're looking for what you offer, and deciding which of those searches each page on your site should answer. Done well, it stops you writing pages nobody searches for and shows you where the easy wins are.

You don't need expensive tools to start. You need your customers' language, a few free tools and a spreadsheet.

## Step 1: Write down your seed keywords

Seed keywords are the basic names for what you sell or do, in the words customers use, not your internal jargon. A saree manufacturer might start with:

- silk saree
- banarasi saree
- saree wholesale

A builder might start with "house construction", "bathroom waterproofing" and "home renovation". Ask your sales team or check WhatsApp enquiries. How customers describe their problem is gold.

## Step 2: Expand each seed

Every seed has dozens of variations. They fall into four groups, and each needs a different kind of page:

- **Local:** "silk saree shop in Surat", "waterproofing contractor near me"
- **Commercial (comparing):** "best banarasi saree brands", "waterproofing vs tiling"
- **Transactional (ready to buy):** "silk saree price", "buy banarasi saree online"
- **Informational (questions):** "how to identify pure silk", "why does my bathroom wall get damp"

The [Rankdesk keyword ideas tool](/keyword-ideas) generates all four groups from your seeds. Then add real searches from these free sources:

- **Google autocomplete:** type your seed slowly and note the suggestions.
- **People also ask:** the question boxes in results show what people want to know next.
- **Related searches:** at the bottom of the results page.
- **Google Search Console:** once your site has some traffic, the Performance report lists searches you already appear for.

## Step 3: Check demand

Not every phrase is worth a page. To see whether people actually search a term:

- **Google Keyword Planner** gives search volume ranges. It's inside Google Ads, but you don't have to run ads to use it.
- **Google Trends** compares the popularity of a few terms and shows seasonal patterns, such as saree searches rising before wedding season.

Small numbers aren't a reason to skip a keyword. Ten searches a month from buyers ready to order can be worth more than a thousand from students.

## Step 4: Judge whether you can rank

Search the keyword yourself and look at page one:

- If it's full of huge national brands and marketplaces, a new site will struggle. Look for a longer, more specific version.
- If you see small local businesses, forums or thin pages, you have a real chance.
- Look at what kind of pages rank. If they're all guides, a product page won't rank there, and the reverse is true too. This is **search intent**, explained in our [search intent guide](/blog/search-intent).

New websites do best with [long-tail keywords](/blog/long-tail-keywords): longer, specific phrases with clear intent.

## Step 5: Group keywords into pages

Put closely related keywords together. "Silk saree price", "pure silk saree cost" and "silk saree rate" are one intent, so they belong on one page. Creating a separate page for each leads to pages competing with each other.

A simple spreadsheet works:

| Page | Main keyword | Related keywords | Intent | Exists? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| /silk-sarees | silk saree | pure silk saree, silk saree price | Transactional | Yes, needs work |
| /blog/identify-pure-silk | how to identify pure silk | silk burn test, real vs fake silk | Informational | No |
| /surat-showroom | saree shop in surat | saree market surat | Local | No |

## Step 6: Use the keyword well on the page

For each page, use the main keyword:

- in the [title tag](/blog/title-tag-guide), near the start
- in the H1 heading
- in the first paragraph, naturally
- in the URL, if you're creating a new page

Use the related keywords in H2 headings and body text where they fit. Don't repeat the main phrase over and over. Modern search understands synonyms and context, and stuffing reads badly.

## Keep it going

Keyword research isn't a one-time job. Every few months, check Search Console for new searches you appear for, add pages for gaps, and improve pages that rank on page two. Small, steady improvements add up.
