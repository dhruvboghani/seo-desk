YouTube is one of the most used search engines in India. People search it for product reviews, how-to guides, recipes and demonstrations before they buy. Videos also appear in Google results, often near the top for "how to" searches. Optimising a video takes a few extra minutes and helps it keep getting views long after you post it.

## Start with what people search

Before filming, check what people actually look for:

- Type your topic into YouTube's search bar and note the autocomplete suggestions.
- Look at the top results. Their titles, lengths and thumbnails show what viewers expect.
- Check Google for "how to" searches on the topic. If Google shows video results, it's a good video topic.

Pick one clear topic per video, matching a real search such as "how to drape a silk saree" or "terrace waterproofing process".

## Titles

The title is the strongest text signal and the main reason people click.

- Put the main search phrase near the start: "How to Drape a Silk Saree in 5 Minutes (Easy Steps)".
- Keep the important part under about 60 characters, as longer titles get cut on phones.
- Promise something specific and deliver it. Misleading titles get clicks but lose viewers fast, which hurts the video.

## Descriptions

- The first two lines show in search and above the "more" link. Use them to describe the video with your main keyword naturally.
- Below that, write a fuller summary of what the video covers, a few paragraphs in plain language.
- Add links to your website, products and related videos.
- Include contact details or your shop address if you serve customers locally.

## Chapters

Chapters split a video into labelled sections, making it easier to watch and helping Google show "key moments" in search. Add timestamps in the description:

```
00:00 Introduction
00:45 Pleating the saree
02:10 Draping the pallu
03:30 Pinning tips
```

YouTube's rules: the first timestamp must be 00:00, you need at least three, and each chapter must be at least 10 seconds long.

## Captions

Upload accurate captions or correct YouTube's automatic ones. Captions help viewers watching without sound and people who are deaf or hard of hearing, and they give YouTube an accurate transcript of your words. If you film in Hindi or Gujarati, add captions in that language, and an English version if your audience is mixed.

## Say your keywords

YouTube can understand what's spoken. Saying "in this video I'll show how to drape a silk saree" early on reinforces the topic.

## Tags and hashtags

**Tags** play a small role. YouTube says they mainly help with common misspellings. Add a few: your main phrase, common variations and your brand. The [hashtag generator](/hashtag-generator) creates a YouTube tag list within the 500-character limit.

**Hashtags** in the description can show above the title. Use a few relevant ones. Adding too many can make YouTube ignore all of them.

## Thumbnails

A clear custom thumbnail affects clicks more than almost anything else: a close-up of the result, a face showing emotion, or a few large words. Make sure it's readable at phone size.

## Keep people watching

YouTube rewards videos people watch and enjoy. Get to the point in the first 15 seconds, cut long pauses, show rather than tell, and end with a link to a related video or playlist.

## Playlists

Group related videos into playlists with keyword-rich titles, such as "Saree Draping Styles". Playlists can rank on their own and keep viewers watching more of your videos.
