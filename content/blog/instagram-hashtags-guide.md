A few years ago, the advice was to fill every caption with thirty hashtags. That era is over. Instagram has been cutting back how many hashtags it allows and says hashtags mainly help it understand what a post is about. Used well, they still help the right people find you. Used badly, they make posts look like spam.

## What hashtags do today

Instagram uses many signals to decide who sees a post: your caption, what's in the image or video, the audio, and how people react. Hashtags are one of those signals. They label the topic, help a post appear on hashtag pages and in search, and group content such as customer photos under a branded tag.

They won't turn a weak post into a viral one. Content people save and share does that.

## How many to use

Instagram has been reducing the maximum number of hashtags per post, and the exact limit has changed over time, so check what the app allows when you post. In practice, **three to five well-chosen hashtags** are enough for most businesses. Relevance matters far more than quantity.

## The four kinds to mix

1. **Niche tags** describe exactly what the post shows: `#banarasisaree`, `#bathroomwaterproofing`, `#egglesscake`. These reach people interested in that topic.
2. **Local tags** reach people nearby: `#suratfashion`, `#ahmedabadfood`, `#vadodarahomes`. Essential for businesses that serve a city.
3. **Branded tags** are yours alone: `#shreetextiles`. Customers can use them too, giving you a collection of real posts.
4. **Community tags** reach a wider but related audience: `#handloomlove`, `#madeinindia`.

A good set for a saree post might be: `#banarasisaree #silksaree #suratfashion #shreetextiles #handloomlove`.

## How to choose good tags

- **Search the tag first.** Look at the top posts. If they don't match your content or audience, skip it.
- **Avoid huge generic tags** like `#love`, `#instagood` or `#fashion`. Millions of posts bury yours instantly, and the audience isn't targeted.
- **Avoid very tiny tags** that nobody uses or searches.
- **Check for banned or broken tags.** Some tags get restricted because of spam. If a tag's page shows only a few top posts or a warning, don't use it.
- **Match the language** your customers use. Hindi, Gujarati or Hinglish tags can reach local audiences with less competition.

## Rotate your sets

Using exactly the same block of hashtags on every post can look automated. Keep three or four sets for your main content types (new arrivals, behind the scenes, customer photos, tips) and use the one that fits each post.

## Where to put them

Hashtags in the caption or the first comment both work, but the caption is the safer choice because it's indexed with the post from the moment it goes live. Put them at the end of the caption, after the real text, so they don't distract readers.

## Hashtags vs keywords

Instagram search now reads captions, names and bios too. A caption like "New Banarasi silk sarees for wedding season" already tells Instagram a lot. Hashtags work alongside keywords, not instead of them. Read our [Instagram SEO guide](/blog/instagram-seo) for the full picture.

## Habits that limit reach

- Copying the same hashtags on every post for months
- Hashtags unrelated to the post, added only because they're popular
- Using engagement pods or follow-for-follow tags
- Buying followers, which damages how Instagram rates your account

## On Reels and YouTube

Reels follow the same rules: a few relevant hashtags plus keywords in the caption and on screen. On YouTube, hashtags in the description can appear above the title, but only the first few are shown and too many can cause YouTube to ignore them all.
