Most small business owners in India hear about SEO from agencies selling monthly packages. Plenty of what those packages do is work you can do yourself, if you know the order. This guide sets out a plan you can follow with free tools and a few hours a week.

## Be realistic about time

SEO is slow. Fixing technical problems and setting up a Google Business Profile can bring results within weeks. Ranking new pages for competitive searches usually takes months. If you need customers this week, run ads alongside. SEO is what keeps bringing them in after the ads stop.

## Week 1: Get found locally

If customers can visit you or you serve a city, this comes first. It's often the fastest return.

1. Claim and complete your **Google Business Profile**. Follow our [setup guide](/blog/google-business-profile-guide).
2. Choose the right primary category and add real photos.
3. Ask your ten most recent happy customers for a Google review.
4. Make sure your name, address and phone are identical on your website, profile, JustDial, IndiaMART and social pages. See [NAP consistency](/blog/nap-consistency).

## Week 2: Fix what blocks your website

Run your home page and main service or product pages through a [free SEO audit](/seo-audit). Fix anything marked critical:

- pages returning errors
- `noindex` tags left over from development
- robots.txt blocking pages
- the site not loading over HTTPS

Then add Google Search Console. It's free, tells you which searches you already appear for, and alerts you to problems.

## Week 3: Write titles and descriptions that sell

Your home page title shouldn't be "Home". Each important page needs a unique [title tag](/blog/title-tag-guide) that says what you offer and where, and a [meta description](/blog/meta-description-examples) that gives a reason to click. This often lifts clicks from searches you already rank for, without ranking any higher.

## Week 4: One page per service or product line

Many small business sites have one page listing everything. Google can't rank one page for twenty different searches. Create a separate page for each main service or product line, with:

- a clear heading that matches how people search
- what it is, who it's for, prices or a price range if you can share them
- photos of your actual work or products
- answers to the questions customers ask on the phone
- a clear way to call or WhatsApp you

A builder might have separate pages for house construction, renovation, waterproofing and interior work. A textile maker might have pages for each fabric or saree style.

## Month 2: Answer customer questions

Write down every question customers ask before buying: price, time, materials, how to choose, how to care for the product. Each good question can become a guide on your website. These pages attract people early in their decision and show you know your trade.

Use the [keyword ideas tool](/keyword-ideas) to find the exact phrasing people search. Then check our [keyword research guide](/blog/keyword-research-for-beginners).

## Month 3 and on: Build reputation

Google trusts websites that other trusted websites mention. As a small business, you earn this through:

- local business directories and trade associations
- suppliers and partners linking to you
- local news coverage of something you did
- guest articles for trade publications

Avoid buying links. It breaks Google's rules, and cheap link packages often do more harm than good.

## Don't forget Instagram and YouTube

Many Indian customers search Instagram and YouTube before Google. Set up your [Instagram profile for search](/blog/instagram-seo) and, if you can, post short videos of your work. The same keywords you found for your website apply there too.

## What to measure

Check these once a month:

- **Search Console:** clicks, impressions and the searches you appear for
- **Google Business Profile:** calls, direction requests and website clicks
- **Enquiries:** ask every new customer how they found you

If the numbers rise slowly, the plan is working. If something drops suddenly, run an audit to see what changed.

## When to hire help

Doing it yourself works well for the basics. Consider an expert when you're in a highly competitive market, when your site has serious technical problems you can't fix, or when your time is worth more running the business. Even then, knowing the basics helps you judge whether an agency is doing real work.
