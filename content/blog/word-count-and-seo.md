"How many words should a page have for SEO?" Ask five people and you'll hear 300, 1,000, 2,000 and "as many as possible". Google has said plainly that word count isn't a ranking factor. Yet thin pages really do struggle. Both are true, and understanding why helps you write the right amount.

## Why there's no magic number

Google ranks pages by how well they meet the searcher's need. Some searches need one line: "Shree Textiles phone number". Others need a detailed guide: "how to start a saree business". Writing 2,000 words for the first wastes everyone's time. Writing 100 words for the second can't possibly cover it.

Long pages often rank well because covering a topic properly usually takes words, not because Google counts them.

## Why thin pages struggle

A page with very little unique content gives Google little reason to show it:

- It may not answer the question fully, so people go back to search.
- It gives search engines few clues about what it covers.
- Hundreds of near-empty pages, like product pages with one line, or city pages with only the city name changed, can make the whole site look low-value.

That's why the [Rankdesk SEO audit](/seo-audit) flags pages with very little visible text. It isn't a rule. It's a prompt to check whether the page says enough.

## How to decide how much to write

**1. Look at what ranks.** Search your keyword and open the top results. If they're all detailed guides, a short page won't compete. If they're product pages with a short description and lots of images, that's the format people want.

**2. List the questions the page should answer.** For a service page: what it is, who it's for, how it works, how long it takes, what it costs, why choose you, what to do next. Answer each one. That sets the length naturally.

**3. Cut what doesn't help.** Long introductions, repeated points and filler to reach a number make a page worse. Readers skim, and they leave when they can't find the answer.

## Rough guide by page type

These aren't targets, just typical ranges for pages that do their job:

- **Contact page:** short. Details, map and directions.
- **Product page:** enough to describe the product fully, such as material, size, use, care and delivery, plus photos and reviews. Often 150 to 400 words of unique text.
- **Service page:** usually 400 to 1,000 words, covering the questions above, with photos of real work.
- **Category page:** a short introduction above the products, and optionally a buying guide below.
- **How-to guide:** as long as the steps require. Often 800 to 2,000 words.

## Quality signals that matter more than length

- **Answers early.** Give the answer near the top, then the detail.
- **First-hand experience.** Real photos, prices, examples, mistakes you've seen. Google's guidance emphasises content that shows real experience and expertise.
- **Structure.** Clear [headings](/blog/heading-structure-h1-h2-h3), short paragraphs, lists and tables make longer pages easy to scan.
- **Accuracy and freshness.** Update prices, laws, dates and screenshots.
- **Unique content.** Copying manufacturer descriptions or competitors' text gives Google no reason to prefer your page.

## Updating short pages

If an important page is thin:

1. Add the questions customers ask about it, with clear answers.
2. Add specifics: prices or ranges, timelines, materials, areas served.
3. Add proof: photos, a short case study, reviews.
4. Add an FAQ section at the end.
5. Link to related guides and pages with [internal links](/blog/internal-linking-guide).

Then use [keyword research](/blog/keyword-research-for-beginners) to check the page covers the related searches people make.

## The short version

Write as much as the reader needs and no more. If you've answered every question a customer would ask, the length is right.
