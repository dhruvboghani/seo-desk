Reviews do two jobs. They're one of the signals Google uses for local rankings, and they're what people read before they call you instead of the shop down the road. Most happy customers never think to leave one. The difference between businesses with 10 reviews and 200 is usually just asking, consistently.

## Get your review link

1. Open your Google Business Profile while signed in.
2. Look for **Ask for reviews** or **Get more reviews**.
3. Copy the short link. When customers open it, the review box appears directly.

Save it somewhere handy, such as a WhatsApp quick reply, your email signature and a QR code at the counter.

## When to ask

Ask at the moment the customer is happiest:

- right after a successful delivery or installation
- when a customer thanks you or says they're happy
- after a problem was solved well
- when a project is handed over

A message sent the same day gets far more reviews than one sent a week later.

## How to ask

Keep it personal and short:

> Thank you for choosing us for your bathroom waterproofing, Mr. Patel! If you're happy with the work, would you share a quick Google review? It helps other families find us: [link]

Some ideas that work:

- **Train your team** to ask in person, then send the link on WhatsApp.
- **Put a QR code** on bills, packaging, the counter and visiting cards.
- **Mention what to write about**, such as the product, service or staff member, so reviews are more useful to others. Don't tell them what rating to give.

## What's not allowed

Google's rules are strict, and breaking them can get reviews removed or your profile restricted:

- **No paying or rewarding for reviews**, including discounts, gifts or contest entries.
- **No review gating:** you can't ask only happy customers, or filter people through a "were you satisfied?" step that sends only the happy ones to Google. You can ask everyone.
- **No fake reviews**, including reviews written by staff, family or friends posing as customers, or bought from review sellers.
- **No reviewing competitors** or asking others to.
- **No reviews from your own premises in bulk**, such as handing customers a shop tablet. Many reviews from one device or network can be filtered.

Google uses automated systems to detect patterns, and suspicious reviews often disappear.

## Replying to reviews

Reply to every review. It shows you care, and future customers read the replies as much as the reviews.

**Positive reviews:** thank the person by name, mention something specific and keep it short.

> Thank you, Riya! We're glad the Banarasi saree was perfect for your sister's wedding. See you next season!

**Negative reviews:** stay calm and professional. Others will judge you by how you respond.

1. Thank them for the feedback.
2. Apologise for the experience, without arguing details in public.
3. Explain briefly what you've done or will do.
4. Offer to continue privately with a phone number or email.

> We're sorry the delivery was late, Mr. Shah. That isn't the standard we aim for. We've changed our dispatch process to stop it happening again. Please call us on 98765 43210 so we can make this right.

## Fake or abusive reviews

If a review breaks Google's policies, for example spam, hate speech, a conflict of interest or a review meant for another business, report it from your Business Profile. Google decides whether to remove it. A review being negative isn't by itself a reason for removal.

## Show reviews on your website

You can display Google reviews on your site with an embed or by quoting them. If you add review [schema](/blog/schema-markup-for-beginners), the reviews must be visible on that page, and Google doesn't show star ratings in results for a business's reviews of itself on its own website.

Reviews are one part of the [local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist-india). Combined with a complete [Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-guide), they make a strong difference in the map results.
