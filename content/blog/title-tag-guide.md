The title tag is the clickable blue headline people see in Google results. It's also the text in the browser tab and the default headline when someone shares your page. Of all the small changes you can make to a page, rewriting a weak title is often the one that makes the biggest difference to clicks.

## Where the title tag lives

It sits in the `<head>` of the page's HTML:

```html
<title>Pure Silk Sarees from Surat | Shree Textiles</title>
```

In WordPress, Shopify, Wix and most builders you don't edit code. You set it in an "SEO title" field, usually under the page settings or in a plugin such as Yoast or Rank Math.

## How long should it be?

Google cuts titles that are wider than about 600 pixels on desktop. For most titles that is around **50 to 60 characters**. Pixels matter more than characters, because wide letters such as W and M take more space than i or l. Write the important words first so a cut never removes them.

Google also rewrites titles that are too long, stuffed with keywords or don't match the page. Keeping yours clear and accurate makes it far more likely Google shows it as written.

## A formula that works

For most pages, use this order:

**Main search phrase + what makes you different + brand**

- `Pure Silk Sarees from Surat | Shree Textiles`
- `Bathroom Waterproofing in Ahmedabad, 5-Year Warranty | Bhanderi Buildcon`
- `Free IP Address Lookup with City and ISP | IPLocator Tools`

The search phrase goes first because people scan the start of each result for the words they typed. The brand goes last because it matters most to people who already know you.

Every "after" tells the searcher what they'll get before they click.

## Rules worth following

- **Make every title unique.** If ten pages share one title, Google can't tell which to show.
- **Match the page.** If the title promises prices, the page should show prices. A mismatch leads to quick back-clicks and Google may rewrite the title.
- **Write for people.** "Saree, sarees, buy saree, saree online" reads as spam. One natural phrase is enough.
- **Use numbers and specifics** where they're true: "5-year warranty", "open Sunday", "12 examples".
- **Separate parts with a pipe `|` or a dash.** Both work. Pick one and use it across the site.

## Titles for different page types

- **Home page:** what you do and where, then the brand. `Interior Designers in Surat for Homes and Offices | Studio Nine`
- **Product page:** the product name as people search it, a key detail, then the brand.
- **Service page:** the service, the city and a differentiator.
- **Blog post:** the question or promise. `How to Remove Hard Water Stains from Tiles`
- **Category page:** the category in plural with a qualifier. `Cotton Kurtis for Women, Under ₹999`

## The title and the H1

The H1 is the main heading on the page itself. It can match the title tag or be a slightly longer, friendlier version. What matters is that both describe the same topic. See our guide to [headings](/blog/heading-structure-h1-h2-h3) for how they fit together.

Before publishing, preview the title as it'll appear in results.
