NAP stands for **Name, Address and Phone number**, the three details that identify a local business online. Every place your business is listed, from your website and Google Business Profile to JustDial, IndiaMART, Facebook and trade directories, should show them the same way.

It sounds minor, but inconsistent details are one of the most common problems we find when auditing local businesses.

## Why it matters

**For search engines:** Google builds a picture of your business from many sources. When your website, profile and directories agree, Google is more confident in the details, which supports your local rankings. When they conflict, such as an old address on one site and a different phone number on another, that confidence drops.

**For customers:** an old number that doesn't connect or an address you moved from three years ago means a lost customer, and often an angry review.

## Where inconsistencies come from

- moving premises and updating only some listings
- changing phone numbers, or using a staff member's personal number
- listings created by customers, data aggregators or former employees
- different name formats: "Shree Textiles", "Shri Textiles", "Shree Textiles Pvt Ltd", "Shree Textile Surat"
- address variations: "Ring Road" vs "Ring Rd", missing PIN codes, different landmarks
- duplicate listings on the same directory

Small formatting differences like "Rd" vs "Road" are usually understood. The serious problems are wrong or outdated details, different names and duplicates.

## Step 1: Decide your official NAP

Write down one version and use it everywhere:

- **Name:** exactly as on your signboard and legal registration, without extra keywords.
- **Address:** one consistent format, including shop number, building, street, area, city, state and PIN code.
- **Phone:** one main number, ideally a landline or a dedicated business mobile, with +91 where the format allows.

## Step 2: Find your listings

Search Google for:

- your business name
- your phone number in quotes, including old numbers
- your old addresses, if you've moved

Then check the directories common in your industry: JustDial, Sulekha, IndiaMART, TradeIndia, Yellow Pages India, Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, Zomato or Practo if relevant, and your trade association directory. Note each listing and what it shows in a spreadsheet.

## Step 3: Fix and merge

- Update every listing you control to the official NAP.
- Claim listings you don't control yet, then update them.
- Ask directories to merge or remove duplicate listings. Most have a "report incorrect information" or support option.
- For Google, suggest edits to wrong details on Maps and mark duplicate profiles for removal.

## Step 4: Make your website the reference

Your website should be the clearest source of your details:

- **NAP in the footer** of every page, as real text (not inside an image).
- **A contact page** with the full address, phone, map and opening hours.
- **[LocalBusiness schema](/blog/local-business-schema)** with exactly the same name, address and phone.
- **Click-to-call phone links** on mobile.

The [schema generator](/schema-generator) pulls your name, phone and address from your website, which is a quick way to spot whether your own pages are consistent.

## Step 5: Keep it consistent

Whenever something changes, update every listing in your spreadsheet the same week. Check once or twice a year for new listings created by others. Many businesses fix their NAP once, then a new branch or phone number undoes it.

## Several locations

Each location needs its own consistent NAP, its own Google Business Profile, and its own page on your website. Don't use one central phone number for every branch on Google if each branch has its own line.
