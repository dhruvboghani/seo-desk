"Saree" is a huge search. Thousands of websites compete for it, including marketplaces with enormous budgets. "Banarasi silk saree for wedding under 10000" is searched far less, but by someone who knows exactly what they want. That second kind of search is a long-tail keyword, and it's where small websites win.

## What makes a keyword long-tail

The name comes from a chart of searches: a few very popular terms at the "head", followed by a long "tail" of millions of specific searches each made less often. Together, the tail accounts for a large share of all searches.

Long-tail keywords are usually:

- **Longer,** often three words or more
- **More specific** about product, place, budget, problem or audience
- **Less competitive,** because fewer pages target them
- **Clearer in intent,** so it's easier to build exactly the right page

Length isn't the real test. "Waterproofing contractor Bopal" is long-tail because it's specific, even though it's only three words.

## Why they suit new and small sites

1. **You can rank sooner.** Big brands rarely build pages for narrow searches.
2. **The traffic converts.** Someone searching "dentist in Vadodara open Sunday" is ready to book.
3. **They add up.** Twenty pages each bringing 30 targeted visitors beat one page stuck on page five for a broad term.
4. **They build toward the head.** As your site gains authority from many specific pages, broader terms become reachable.

## Kinds of long-tail keywords

- **Product plus detail:** "pure silk saree with silver zari"
- **Product plus place:** "saree wholesale market Surat"
- **Product plus price or budget:** "kitchen sink under 5000"
- **Problem-based:** "bathroom wall damp after rain"
- **Questions:** "how long does waterproofing last"
- **Audience:** "accounting software for small shops"
- **Comparison:** "membrane vs chemical waterproofing"

## How to find them

- **Google autocomplete:** type your main keyword and add each letter of the alphabet after it.
- **People also ask** and **related searches** on the results page.
- **Your customers' words:** WhatsApp chats, emails and phone calls are full of real long-tail searches.
- **Search Console:** the Performance report lists long searches you already appear for, often on page two. These are your quickest wins.
- **The [keyword ideas tool](/keyword-ideas):** expands each seed into local, buying, comparison and question versions.

## How to target them without creating thin pages

A common mistake is building a separate page for every small variation. "Silk saree price", "silk saree rate" and "silk saree cost" mean the same thing and belong on one page.

Group long-tail keywords by [intent](/blog/search-intent):

- If they want the same thing, cover them on **one page**, using the variations in headings and text.
- If they want different things, give them **separate pages**.

Questions often fit best as sections of a larger guide or in an FAQ at the bottom of a service page.

## Writing for a long-tail keyword

- Put the phrase, or a natural version of it, in the [title tag](/blog/title-tag-guide), the H1 and the opening paragraph.
- Answer the specific question quickly. Someone searching "how long does waterproofing last" wants a number early, followed by the detail.
- Add the details the search implies: prices for "under 10000", areas and directions for local searches, steps for "how to".
- Link to your related product or service pages so readers can act.

## A simple plan

1. Pick your main product or service.
2. Collect 30 to 50 long-tail variations.
3. Group them by intent into 5 to 10 page ideas.
4. Improve existing pages first, then write the missing ones, one a week.
5. After a few months, check Search Console for new long-tail searches you appear for, and repeat.
