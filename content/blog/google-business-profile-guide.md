When someone nearby searches for "saree shop near me" or "plumber in Navrangpura", the first thing they usually see is a map with three businesses under it. Those listings come from Google Business Profile, a free listing that every shop, clinic, office and service business should claim.

For many local businesses, the profile brings more calls and visits than the website does. Here is how to set it up properly.

## Step 1: Claim or create the profile

1. Go to google.com/business and sign in with the Google account you want to manage the business from. Use a business account rather than a personal one if several people will help.
2. Search for your business name. If it already exists (customers sometimes add businesses to Maps), claim it. If not, create it.
3. Choose whether customers visit you at a location, you visit them, or both. A business that serves customers at their address, like a carpenter, can hide its street address and show a service area instead.

## Step 2: Verify it

Google needs to confirm you run the business before the profile goes live in full. It offers whichever methods it considers right for your business, which can include a video recording of your premises and signage, a phone or SMS code, email, or a postcard. Follow the instructions exactly. A video, for example, usually needs to show your signboard, the location and proof you manage it, such as opening the shop.

Don't change your name, address or category while verification is pending, as it can restart the process.

## Step 3: Get the core details right

- **Business name:** your real name as it appears on your signboard. Adding keywords, such as "Shree Textiles Best Saree Shop Surat", breaks Google's rules and can get the profile suspended.
- **Primary category:** the single most important ranking setting. Pick the most specific category that describes your main business, such as "Saree shop" rather than "Clothing store". Add a few secondary categories for other services.
- **Address and pin:** check that the map pin sits exactly on your entrance.
- **Phone and website:** use a number that is answered during business hours.
- **Hours:** include special hours for Diwali and other holidays. Wrong hours lead to angry reviews.

Use exactly the same name, address and phone number everywhere online. Our [NAP consistency guide](/blog/nap-consistency) explains why.

## Step 4: Fill everything else

Profiles with more detail tend to get more engagement:

- **Description:** 750 characters explaining what you do, who for and where. Write it for customers, naturally.
- **Products and services:** add each main service with a short description and, where sensible, a price.
- **Attributes:** for example wheelchair access, women-owned, online appointments or delivery.
- **Photos:** your shopfront from the street (so people recognise it), inside, your team, products and finished work. Real photos beat stock images. Add new ones every month.

## Step 5: Reviews

Reviews influence both rankings and whether people choose you. Ask every happy customer, make it easy with your review link, and reply to every review, good or bad. Our [guide to getting more Google reviews](/blog/google-reviews-guide) covers what is allowed and what is not.

## Step 6: Keep it active

- **Posts:** share offers, new stock, events and updates. They appear on your profile and show you're active.
- **Questions and answers:** customers can ask questions on your profile. Answer them quickly and accurately.
- **Messages and calls:** respond fast. Slow replies lose customers to the next listing.

## Connect it to your website

Your profile and website support each other. Link the profile to the most relevant page, usually your home page or a location page. On the website, show the same name, address and phone, embed a map on the contact page, and add [LocalBusiness schema](/blog/local-business-schema) so search engines can match the two.

## Common mistakes

- Keyword-stuffed business names
- Wrong primary category
- A virtual office or PO box as the address (not allowed)
- Several profiles for one location
- Photos that are only logos or stock images
- Never replying to reviews

Fix these and work through the [local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist-india), and your profile will be in a strong position against competitors who set theirs up once and forgot about it.
